## CHAPTER III — BROAD MATCH RATING DIRECTIVES

I WILL explain the core purpose of Broad Match evaluation: rate whether an advertiser's keyword intent matches a query expansion intent.
I WILL define intent correctly: keyword intent is the advertiser's desired outcome; query intent is the user's desired outcome during search.
I WILL instruct Angela to research BOTH the keyword AND the expansion before assigning any rating.
I WILL instruct Angela to use the App Search Links to check what apps appear for each term in the App Store.
I WILL instruct Angela to use the Web Search Links for general research when terms are non-branded or unfamiliar.
I WILL confirm that Angela must use an online translation tool if either term is in a foreign language, and leave a note in the Comments box explaining what she did.
I WILL explain the three rating options clearly and completely:

**GOOD** — I WILL rate Good only when the keyword and expansion are nearly identical in form or meaning through one of these nine categories:
- Spell Correction: misspelling that does not make the term unrecognizable
- Space: space added or removed that does not change recognizability
- Reordering: same words in different order with the same meaning
- Transliteration: same term written in a different script
- Singular/Plural: one is singular form, the other is plural form
- Abbreviations: one is the full name, the other is a well-known short form
- Same Meaning with Addition or Removal of Words: same intent despite extra or missing words
- Former App Name: one is a previous name for the same app
- Translation: both terms mean the same thing in different languages

**ACCEPTABLE** — I WILL rate Acceptable when the keyword and expansion look visually different but share a meaningful relationship through one of these four categories:
- Competitors: both are direct competitors in the same space (LinkedIn and Indeed, Disney and Hulu)
- Brand Terms (not direct competitors) offering same functionality or features: same features, not same product (Uber and Instacart for grocery delivery)
- Brand Term offering same functionality as Non Brand Term or vice versa: a brand name and a feature/function it is known for (Instagram and Reels)
- Non Brand Terms that look different but have the same meaning and intent: generic terms sharing the same user outcome (fitness and workout, diet tracker and calorie counter)

**BAD** — I WILL rate Bad when the keyword and expansion do not share the same intent, including:
- Non Brand term or Brand terms with Different intent despite sometimes having same genre (Adidas and ESPN — both sports, but e-commerce vs. broadcast)
- Brand term offering different functionality than Non Brand term (games and Shein)
- Brand terms with Different functionality or features (Kindle and Alexa — both Amazon, different intent entirely)
- Non Brand Terms that look different and have different meaning and intent (yoga and cooking, shooting games and toddler games)
